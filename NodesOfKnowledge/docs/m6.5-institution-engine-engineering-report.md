## 0. Nature of this revision (an honesty statement)

Constitution v1.1 was adopted **after** M6.5 was implemented. It added the Design
Bible's Part VI (Chapter 17, "Originality by Understanding," and Design Principle
17) and the Product Blueprint's Part XIV ("The Benchmarking Discipline"). M6.5 was
therefore **not** consciously designed by executing v1.1's nine-step benchmarking
workflow — that workflow did not yet exist. This report does not pretend otherwise.

What this revision does is audit whether the architectural decisions *already made*
in M6.5 naturally satisfy the principles v1.1 later formalized. The distinction
that makes this honest is one v1.1 itself draws: v1.1's **substantive** first
principles — Node Independence, provenance-first, temporal honesty, institutional
sovereignty, equal dignity, narrative/evidence separation — are **not new**; they
pre-existed in the Constitution and governed M6.5 at implementation time. What v1.1
genuinely *added* is the benchmarking **discipline** — a method and a governance
hierarchy for *how* future milestones approach design. So the audit has two halves:
against the pre-existing substantive principles, M6.5 followed already-settled law;
against the newly-formalized benchmarking discipline, M6.5's outcomes are assessed
retrospectively.

**Verdict:** the audit found **no genuine constitutional inconsistency**. Therefore
**no production code changes are required; M6.5 already satisfies Constitution
v1.1; only this engineering report was expanded** to document the constitutional
relationship. Nothing was committed, pushed, or tagged.

## 2. Benchmarking Synthesis

This section is written *retrospectively* and honestly: it reconstructs the problem
landscape M6.5's decisions relate to, and shows the synthesis is original NoK work —
while making no claim that a formal benchmarking exercise produced the architecture.

**The problem landscape that informed the milestone.** The M6.5 charter itself named
the neighbouring infrastructures — persistent identifier registries (ROR, Wikidata,
ISNI, VIAF, GRID), museum and archival descriptive traditions, open biodiversity
informatics, knowledge graphs, and research/scientific cyberinfrastructure — and it
named, as an explicit prohibition, the temptation to build "a weaker version of" an
organization registry, Wikipedia/Wikidata, a museum collections portal, an
institutional website, a biodiversity occurrence database, a university directory,
an archival catalogue, or a CRM. That framing *is* the problem landscape; the
implementation engaged it in the mode v1.1 later mandated — asking what problem each
system recognised, not how it solved it.

**The design principles extracted (problems, not solutions).** Registries recognised
the *institutional disambiguation* problem — the same-named, same-acronym, relocated
institution — and the value of interoperable external identity. Encyclopaedias
recognised the need for a *canonical current entity* and a readable narrative. Museum
and archival traditions recognised *statement-level description*, *custody/provenance
chains*, and *honest absence* as first-class. Biodiversity informatics recognised
*interoperable metadata and reuse* in service of decisions. Knowledge graphs
recognised *entity-to-entity connectivity*. University directories recognised the
need to *organise people* around an institution. Each recognised a real problem M6.5
must also understand.

**The architectural patterns deliberately rejected.** Every one of these systems also
encodes an assumption made for someone else's purpose, and M6.5 refuses them
consciously: *identifier-as-authority / silent auto-merge on identifier match*
(rejected — `unique(scheme, identifier_value)` guarantees one value maps to one
institution, but never merges records, and an external id never defines the
institution's history); *redirecting a predecessor into its successor* (rejected —
historical/merged institutions stay readable and separate; a successor inherits
nothing automatically); *record-level-only provenance* (rejected — provenance is
statement-level, on every name, identifier, facet, event, and participation);
*narrative-as-fact / mission-statement-as-history* (rejected — narrative is a
separate, provenance-bearing, never-generated facet set); *the prestige hierarchy,
staff directory, or leaderboard* (rejected — participation is grouped by capacity and
ordered historically); *the raw file or dataset repository* (rejected under CC2 —
Historical Records is reserved as knowledge-making history, not custody of files);
*the monolithic entity RPC and the generic graph edge* (rejected — bounded reads, and
canonical-record projection rather than edge duplication); and *a parallel
`institutions` identity table* (rejected — additive extension of `organizations`).

**The original synthesis adopted by Nodes of Knowledge.** From those understood
problems and the Constitution's primitives, M6.5 synthesises answers that belong to no
studied system: an **additive institutional identity** grown from the existing entity;
**canonical-record projection** (Many-Clocks) so one Event or Participation appears on
the person's and the institution's clocks with no contradictory copy; **statement-level
provenance and temporal qualification** on every institutional assertion;
**transformation modelled as a combination** of Events, historical names, status, and
a deferred relationship generalisation — never a single mutable field, and never at the
cost of a predecessor's separate history; **sovereignty without a monopoly on truth**,
carried by per-assertion provenance rather than an assigned owner; and **bounded,
independently-evolving reads** behind a deny-by-default boundary.

**Why it advances beyond existing infrastructures while remaining faithful to the
Constitution.** No single benchmarked system offers this combination: registries lack
narrative and statement-level provenance; encyclopaedias flatten predecessors and blend
narrative with fact; biodiversity databases centre raw data the Constitution's CC2
excludes; knowledge graphs dissolve bonds into generic edges; directories rank by
status. M6.5 unites what those systems separate — identity, history, people,
provenance, and institutional transformation — as the mission requires, and it does so
*within* the settled architecture, extending it additively rather than importing a
foreign shape. This is precisely Principle 17's test met in outcome: originality as the
understanding of prior work deeply enough to design past it.

**The honest caveat.** Benchmarking-as-problem-framing genuinely *refined* the
implementation — it sharpened the refusals above and deepened the understanding of the
disambiguation, sovereignty, and provenance problems. It did **not** *determine* the
architecture: what to build came from the ratified M6.5 goals and the pre-existing
constitutional primitives, which sit *above* benchmarking in v1.1's hierarchy. And M6.5
did not run the formal nine-step workflow, because that workflow postdates it. Every
major decision above is an original NoK synthesis or a conscious *rejection* of an
imitation pattern — not an adaptation of any platform's schema, interface, or workflow.

## 3. Implementation decisions vs. later-ratified v1.1 principles

- **Made during M6.5 implementation (governed by the pre-v1.1 Constitution and the
  ratified M6.5 goals):** additive extension of `organizations`; canonical-record
  projection (`organization_events`; the org-side participation read); three bounded
  `SECURITY DEFINER` reads; statement-level provenance on names/identifiers/facets;
  institution status as a fixed CHECK vocabulary and types as a lookup table;
  historical institutions readable; transformation via Events+names+status; deferral of
  institution↔institution relationships; equal-dignity capacity grouping; the
  `/institutions/[organizationId]` route and the Participation discovery link.
- **Ratified later, in v1.1, and now providing governance:** the benchmarking
  *discipline* itself — Design Bible Part VI / Design Principle 17 (benchmark for
  problems, not solutions; benchmarking beneath the Constitution; no reopening settled
  questions), and Product Blueprint Part XIV (the Constitution → Vision → Ratified Goals
  → Benchmarking → Synthesis → Implementation hierarchy, the nine-step workflow, and the
  never-redefine list). These formalise the *method*; they did not alter any substantive
  decision M6.5 had already made, and this audit confirms none needs altering.
