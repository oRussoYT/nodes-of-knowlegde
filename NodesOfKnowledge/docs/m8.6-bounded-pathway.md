# M8.6 — Revelation Engine: bounded pathway (C6)

Status: implemented (pending local Mac validation). See
`docs/decisions/0018-revelation-engine.md` (amended for M8.6), the M8 Definitive
Specification (§3.2 bounded multi-hop composition + the endpoint rule, §6.6
boundedness, §4 no-metric), and the M8 Engineering Blueprint (C6, line 61). This
is the **final** revelation capability.

## What this is

M8.1–M8.5 each read from a **single** focal record. M8.6 is the first
**two-endpoint** lens: given a focal person and a **selected target entity**, it
reveals a *finite, small, fully-visible* **chain of explicit assertions**
connecting them through intermediaries — a documented pathway.

- **`reveal_person_pathway(p_from, p_to)`** — on `/people/[personId]`, read when a
  target is chosen via `?pathwayTo=<id>`: the shortest documented chain of **≥ 2**
  explicit‑assertion steps from the focal person to the target, over the
  heterogeneous canonical graph, bounded to a **small hop cap (4)** and cycle‑safe,
  each step decomposable to its canonical row with both endpoints as `ProjectedNode`
  doorways.

## The endpoint rule (Spec §3.2) — this milestone's governing law

A pathway asserts **nothing** about its endpoints beyond the literal existence of
the chain. The surface states **"a documented chain of N steps connects A and B"**
and **never** "A is connected to B." A long chain conveys a felt connection the
evidence does not support, so the hop bound is kept **small** and longer chains are
treated with **more** reserve, not less. The chain's length is a **fact**, never a
rank; pathways are never ordered or scored by length; no path‑significance,
closeness, or centrality is computed.

## The heterogeneous graph (ratified with the product owner)

Nodes are the four canonical entity types; edges are the explicit,
provenance‑bearing assertions that connect two entities, read **undirected**:
`relationships` (person–person), `organization_relationships` (org–org),
`participations` (person–org), `person_contributions` / `organization_contributions`
(entity–contribution), and `person_events` / `organization_events` /
`contribution_events` (entity–event). An event‑association step carries the event
record's own provenance/temporal (the bare link is the explicit placement; the
event is the documented record it points at).

## ≥ 2 steps only — no overlap with M7 or M8.3

A single explicit assertion linking two entities is M7's one‑hop **connection**,
already read on each record's page. The pathway lens is strictly **multi‑hop**: it
reveals only chains of two or more steps, so it never re‑presents (nor competes
with) an M7 connection — a direct link yields an honest `found:false` ("a direct
link is a connection, not a pathway"). It also composes **across kinds**, so it
never re‑presents M8.3's same‑kind directional descent.

## The deterministic rule

A bounded (hop cap 4), cycle‑safe, breadth‑ordered traversal over the undirected
edge set carries each step's decomposable payload; the single minimal‑length chain
(≥ 2 steps) is chosen by `(depth, neutral path key)` — never by any metric. Each
step carries its canonical `source`, both endpoint `ProjectedNode`s in traversal
order, the connecting assertion's `category` + vocabulary `label`, its `temporal`,
and its `provenance`. Merged people are excluded from the graph and as endpoints; a
merged/nonexistent focal → null; an unresolvable target → an honest "not found"
document; no chain within the bound → an honest absence, never "not connected".

## Reader experience (no destination of its own)

The lens is parameterised by `?pathwayTo`. With no target it shows a calm "choose
an entity to trace a documented chain" state — **no picker, no candidate list**
(that would be recommendation). The target is chosen from a **minimal doorway**
("Trace the documented chain to this person") added to people already revealed on
the page (the M8.1 cohort members). There is no discovery console and no graph
view; the chain is fully readable and decomposable as accessible, textual, ordered
steps.

## Feature module (reuse)

Reuses `parse-shared` (`parseAnyNode`, `parseTemporal`, provenance),
`ProjectedNode`, `RevealedPeriod`, `ProvenanceAffordance`, and the `revelationCopy`
module. Adds `parse-pathway.ts` (fail‑closed, with a chain **contiguity** guarantee
— a broken or partial chain is never shown), the pathway types, `read`/`derive`/
`copy` lenses, and two components (`PersonPathway`, `PathwayStepList`). The
migration adds one internal `pathway_entity_node(text,uuid)` helper (revoked from
public) plus `reveal_person_pathway`.

## Future compatibility with M9

M9 (interpretation) is where meaning belongs. M8.6 hands M9 a clean, decomposable
substrate: a chain is an ordered list of canonical assertion ids with their
provenance, asserting nothing about its endpoints. M9 may interpret whether a
documented chain is *significant* or what it *means*; M8.6 reveals only *that* the
record composes it, and how many steps — leaving every judgement to the reader and
to M9. This completes M8: the platform now **reveals** every documented collective
pattern the specification named, and infers, ranks, and interprets nothing.

## Tests

pgTAP (`revelation-pathway.test.sql`, 24 assertions: heterogeneous 3‑step chain in
traversal order; the ≥ 2‑step rule; contribution/organization targets; the small
hop bound enforced (a 4‑step line found, a 5‑step line absent); ProjectedNode
contract + canonical‑source decomposition; merged exclusion; honest absence
(disconnected, unresolved); fail‑closed auth; determinism). Developed and validated
against a disposable Postgres 16 with the full schema before delivery. Vitest
(parse/derive/copy: contiguity + count + ≥2 fail‑closed, the four states, the
endpoint‑rule summary, banned vocabulary). Playwright (`revelation-pathway.spec.ts`:
the chain inline under the endpoint rule, the doorway affordance, the four states,
a11y/375px/dark/console).
