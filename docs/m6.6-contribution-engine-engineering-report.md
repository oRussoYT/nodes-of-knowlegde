# M6.6 — Contribution Engine — Definitive Engineering Report

## 1. Executive verdict

M6.6 is implemented as the smallest durable Contribution Engine: Contribution
as a first-class historical object, projected consistently onto a person, an
institution, and its own dedicated page from one canonical set of records. It
adds one additive migration, a bounded SECURITY DEFINER read model, a
server-first feature module, full pgTAP / Vitest / Playwright coverage, ADR-0016,
and documentation. It reuses the shared temporal and provenance kernels
verbatim, projects canonical Events without duplication, keeps kind and capacity
as distinct axes, gives every attribution its own provenance, keeps narrative
separate from evidence, represents collective and Indigenous contributions
without fabricating individuals, and introduces no ranking, score, impact, or
credit share. Benchmarking refined implementation but did not determine or
reopen architecture. Nothing was committed, pushed, or tagged. Local validation
on the Mac remains authoritative (see §53).

## 2. Milestone purpose

Conventional scientific records privilege named authors, final outputs, formal
institutions, citations, and current status, and lose the labour and knowledge
that make science possible. M6.6 makes that visible — what people, institutions,
communities, and knowledge traditions made possible, and how we know — without
fabricating credit, extracting knowledge, flattening collective action, or
turning history into metrics, and while preserving equal dignity across forms of
contribution.

## 3. Constitutional principles governing M6.6

Entity/Assertion/Provenance; CC1 (participation, relationship, event, and
contribution are distinct primitives, never one generic model); CC2 (a history
of knowledge-making, not a repository); Node Independence; institutional
sovereignty; equal dignity; temporal honesty (Many-Clocks); narrative/evidence
separation; deny-by-default security; bounded read architecture; benchmarking
subordinate to the Constitution; the prohibition on redefining settled
questions. These governed the milestone and were treated as settled law.

# Benchmarking Synthesis

## Problem landscape studied

Contributor attribution and authorship (CRediT, publication contribution
statements); scholarly metadata and persistent identifiers (Crossref, DataCite,
ORCID, OpenAlex, Wikidata); research-resource and data citation; funding and
institutional attribution; museum and archival description (CIDOC CRM, archival
authority records, museum catalogues); scientific collections and biodiversity
informatics; evidence-based conservation portals; Indigenous data and knowledge
governance (CARE principles, Local Contexts / Traditional Knowledge labels as a
problem statement, not an implementation); oral history; digital humanities;
science and technology studies; research infrastructure; labor history; and
community-based conservation.

## Problems discovered

Publication-centered systems make authorship the unit of credit and thereby
lose non-authorship labour. Role taxonomies (CRediT) encode a fixed set of
publication roles and invite prestige ordering and share-splitting. Persistent
identifiers disambiguate but tempt systems to treat an external id as authority.
Impact and citation metrics convert history into a ranking and reward
visibility, not contribution. Museum/archival models describe objects well but
subordinate the people and communities whose knowledge the objects encode.
Collective and Indigenous knowledge is routinely flattened into a single
"author" or an institution, or acknowledged in small type without governance —
and acknowledgment is treated as permission. Funding and hosting are conflated
with intellectual ownership. Contribution is repeatedly conflated with output,
with the date of an output, and with later consequence or effectiveness.

## Principles extracted

Separate *what was contributed* (a historical object) from *how each actor
helped* (a capacity), and both from *whether it was confirmed* (verification).
Make each attribution its own provenance-bearing assertion. Keep a contribution's
time its own, distinct from any output or event. Preserve equal dignity
structurally — no shares, no ranking, no prestige ordering. Let collective and
unnamed contribution be a first-class state, not a fabricated individual. Treat
external identifiers, outputs, funding, and consequence as related-but-distinct,
never as the contribution itself. Record a historical role without claiming
ownership of knowledge or unauthorized community authorization, and never expose
restricted knowledge.

## Patterns deliberately rejected

CRediT's fixed authorship-role taxonomy and any share/percentage model;
DataCite/Crossref/ORCID/OpenAlex/Wikidata identifier-as-authority and
output-as-record modelling; CIDOC CRM's object-centric and unconstrained-graph
generality; museum-catalogue and grant-report framings; citation graphs, impact
factors, effectiveness scores, and leaderboards; a universal polymorphic
attribution edge. Their implementations were forgotten; only the problems and
trade-offs were carried forward.

## Original Nodes of Knowledge synthesis

A canonical `contributions` object with its own temporal scope and provenance;
two distinct data-backed vocabularies (`contribution_kinds` for the object,
`contribution_capacities` for the manner of helping); explicit, typed
`person_contributions` / `organization_contributions` attributions, each its own
assertion, never inferred and never polymorphic; a separate
`contribution_narrative`; `contribution_events` projecting canonical Events
without duplication; and four bounded SECURITY DEFINER reads that project one
canonical set of records onto the person, institution, and dedicated pages so
they are consistent by construction. Collective/Indigenous contribution is
representable without a fabricated person; equal dignity is enforced by the
schema and the read ordering.

## Why this advances the state of the art

It makes non-authorship, collective, and infrastructural knowledge first-class
history with per-attribution provenance, rather than acknowledgements in small
type; it separates kind, capacity, and verification where prevailing systems
conflate authorship, credit, and impact; it refuses metrics and share-splitting
by construction; and it holds a truthful account even when the record is
incomplete, collective, disputed, unequal, or still unfolding — the explicit
measure of success in the milestone brief.

## Why benchmarking refined implementation but did not determine architecture

The architecture derives from the Constitution (Entity/Assertion/Provenance,
CC1/CC2, equal dignity, temporal honesty, Node Independence) and the ratified
M6.1–M6.5 engines. Benchmarking supplied the problem landscape — which
distinctions matter, which conflations to avoid — and thereby refined the kind
and capacity vocabularies and the collective-contribution boundary. It did not
select the schema, the read model, or the security model, and it reopened
nothing settled.

## 5. Semantic definition of Contribution

A historically situated, provenance-bearing account of something an actor helped
bring into being, sustain, transform, preserve, understand, transmit, or make
possible — recorded as a canonical object, distinct from and never inferred from
participation, relationship, event, output, narrative, or evidence.

## 6. Contribution vs. Participation

Participation (M6.3) is bounded belonging. A contribution is never inferred from
affiliation, employment, or a participation row; the two share no derivation
path in schema or reads (verified by the "no inference from participation" e2e
test).

## 7. Contribution vs. Relationship

A Relationship (M6.4) is a bond between entities that may enable a contribution;
the bond is not the contribution. Mentorship remains an M6.4 Relationship and is
deliberately not a contribution kind.

## 8. Contribution vs. Event

An Event (M6.2) is something that happened. A contribution has its own temporal
scope and is not reducible to an event; canonical Events are *projected* via
`contribution_events` without duplication, and a contribution's time is never
automatically an event date.

## 9. Contribution vs. Output

An output (publication, dataset, specimen, protocol, map, software, policy
document) may be evidence for, an output of, or a referenced entity of a
contribution, but a contribution is not modelled as an output (CC2). Outputs and
records are a reserved later engine.

## 10. Contribution vs. Narrative and Evidence

Narrative is interpretation, kept in `contribution_narrative` with its own
provenance; it does not prove a contribution occurred. Evidence supports an
attribution; the contribution record's provenance does not automatically prove
any attribution — each attribution carries its own.

## 11. Original architecture

One canonical `contributions` record; two distinct data-backed vocabularies
(kind vs. capacity); explicit typed `person_contributions` /
`organization_contributions` attributions; a separate `contribution_narrative`;
`contribution_events` projecting canonical Events; and four bounded SECURITY
DEFINER reads composed at three pages. Consistency across surfaces is guaranteed
by construction (one projected set of records), not by synchronization.

## 12. ADR-0016 decisions

Recorded in `docs/decisions/0016-contribution-engine.md`: the semantic
boundary; canonical identity with its own temporal scope and provenance; kind
vs. capacity as two axes; explicit typed (non-polymorphic) attribution with
per-attribution provenance; collective/Indigenous representability without
fabrication; narrative separate from assertion; events projected not duplicated
with the relation vocabulary deferred; bounded read-model; deny-by-default
security; and the deferred universal-Entity / generalized-attribution path.

## 13. Database migration

`supabase/migrations/20260808090000_add_contribution_engine.sql` — one additive
migration. It weakens no prior migration and adds no Contribution field to the
Biography or Institution identity RPCs.

## 14. Tables and associations

`contribution_kinds`, `contribution_capacities` (vocabularies); `contributions`
(canonical record); `person_contributions`, `organization_contributions`
(explicit typed attributions, `unique(contribution_id, actor_id, capacity)`);
`contribution_narrative` (facets, one per kind); `contribution_events`
(canonical Event projection, `unique(contribution_id, event_id)`). Every new
table: RLS enabled, no client policy, writes granted only to `service_role`,
justified indexes, `updated_at` triggers where an `updated_at` exists.

## 15. Contribution-kind vocabulary

28 seeded, Node-neutral kinds naming *what* was contributed (empirical
observation, field knowledge, taxonomic knowledge, ecological interpretation,
conceptual contribution, research question, method or protocol, technical
adaptation, long-term monitoring, data stewardship, collection stewardship,
field infrastructure, institutional infrastructure, archival preservation,
oral-history preservation, training, translation, logistical knowledge,
community governance, local/Indigenous knowledge, conservation practice,
management model, policy influence, scientific communication, software/tooling,
publication/report, referenced dataset, other). Accepted because each is a
genuinely different historical object. Rejected as kinds: "author"/authorship
roles (publication-centered, CRediT); "mentorship" (an M6.4 Relationship);
"funding"/"hosting" (capacities). Extensible as data by a Node.

## 16. Contributor-capacity vocabulary

18 seeded capacities naming *how* a contributor helped (conceptual development,
field observation, site knowledge, protocol development, technical construction,
data stewardship, analysis, documentation, coordination, training, community
governance, custodianship, archival preservation, translation, logistical
support, institutional support, funding, other). A distinct axis from kind;
funding and institutional support are capacities, never kinds and never
ownership. No prestige ordering, no shares.

## 17. Person attribution

`person_contributions`: contribution + person + capacity + optional attribution
note + non-prestige `sort_order` + its own provenance. Never inferred from
authorship, affiliation, employment, participation, event co-occurrence, or
metadata. `unique(contribution_id, person_id, capacity)` allows a person several
distinct capacities on one contribution, never a duplicate.

## 18. Institutional attribution

`organization_contributions`: same shape from the institution's side. A funder
is an attributed contributor with a `funding` capacity, a host with
`institutional_support` — never intellectual ownership, never inferred from
affiliation, address, event hosting, participation, or adjacent funding.

## 19. Collective and Indigenous contribution boundary

A contribution requires no individual contributor; both attribution tables may
be empty. A collective is attributed to a community or Indigenous organization
(M6.5 types) or carried in narrative with an honest limitation — never a
fabricated person (verified by the "collective contribution" e2e test and the
parse unit test). The platform records a historical role; it never claims
ownership of knowledge, never claims unauthorized community authorization, and
never exposes culturally restricted, sacred, private, or sensitive knowledge.
Community-authorization governance is a documented deferred extension point, not
resolved in code.

## 20. Temporal model

The shared M6.2 Many-Clocks kernel, reused verbatim (precision / approximation /
uncertainty / missing + intervals + open-ended), with the identical CHECK
discipline as `events` / `participations` / `relationships`. A contribution's
temporal scope is its own and is never automatically a publication, event,
participation, or founding date. Undated, approximate, uncertain, and ongoing
are first-class honest states.

## 21. Narrative model

`contribution_narrative` holds curated, human-authored facets (overview /
context / significance / legacy), one per facet, each with provenance and an
`updated_at` trigger. Never auto-generated or AI; never a publication abstract
or self-description passed off as history. A missing facet is an honest absence.

## 22. Event association

Implemented as `contribution_events` — a pure projection of canonical M6.2
Events with no duplication (mirrors `person_events` / `organization_events`),
read via `get_contribution_timeline` and rendered by the reused M6.2 Timeline
components. A relation-type vocabulary (originated / announced / implemented /
recognized / continued) is deliberately deferred: it is not required for the
first complete reading experience and would fork the shared timeline read shape.
Association never implies causation. The dedicated page renders the related-events
surface only when events are present (no empty decorative section).

## 23. Output/reference boundary

No outputs, records, datasets, publications, or citation graph are modelled or
ingested (CC2). Publications/datasets/specimens are, at most, evidence for or
referenced entities of a contribution; a "Records" surface on the dedicated page
is an honest reserved state describing what will appear with its own custody and
provenance.

## 24. Funding and hosting boundary

Funding and hosting are capacities on an institutional attribution, never
kinds, never inferred, never intellectual ownership. Contributor, host, funder,
custodian, and beneficiary are not collapsed into one generic association;
finer distinctions beyond capacity are deferred.

## 25. Conservation-significance boundary

Significance is a narrative facet documented from evidence, never derived from
citation counts, prestige, or later fame. Adoption, implementation, influence,
consequence, effectiveness, and later interpretation are distinct from the
contribution and reserved (a "Consequences" surface), never asserted as caused
outcomes. There is no impact score and no "successful contribution" state.

## 26. Read-model architecture

`get_contribution`, `get_contribution_timeline`, `get_person_contributions`,
`get_organization_contributions` — four bounded reads composed at the page, each
evolving independently. No mega-RPC; no Contribution field on Biography or
Institution identity RPCs.

## 27. Security model

Deny-by-default: RLS enabled on all seven new tables with no client policy;
table writes granted only to `service_role`; the four reads are SECURITY
DEFINER with `search_path = public, pg_temp`, `auth.uid()` required, EXECUTE
revoked from PUBLIC and granted to `authenticated`. Merged people are omitted;
merged/historical institutions remain readable (M6.5). No direct client write
path was introduced to simplify tests.

## 28. Dedicated Contribution page

`/contributions/[contributionId]` — protected, Node-neutral generic route,
generic `<title>`. Reading flow: identity (title h1; kind · own scope · place;
description; provenance), overview + historical context, contributors by
capacity, institutional context, related events (only when present),
significance, legacy, and honest reserved Records / Consequences surfaces. It
reads as a scholarly object history / archival dossier, not a publication page,
grant report, project card, impact dashboard, or leaderboard.

## 29. Person projection

`/people/[personId]` — the reserved "Scientific contributions" section is now
the live `PersonContributions` projection: what the person helped make possible,
in what capacity, when, with the attribution's own provenance, each linking to
its dedicated page. Driven by `get_person_contributions`.

## 30. Institution projection

`/institutions/[organizationId]` — the reserved "Scientific contributions"
section is now the live `InstitutionContributions` projection from the
institution's perspective, driven by `get_organization_contributions`.
Consistent with the person and dedicated pages by construction.

## 31. Empty, incomplete, disputed, and unknown states

Honest throughout: "No account recorded yet" (absent narrative), "Contributors
are not individually recorded" (collective/unnamed), "No contributions recorded
yet" (person/institution with none), "Date unknown" (undated scope), and calm
rendering of provisional/disputed provenance. No fabricated data, no decorative
empty sections, no counts-as-status.

## 32. Provenance and verification

Every assertion — the contribution record, each person attribution, each
organization attribution, and each narrative facet — carries `source_type` and
`verification_status`, rendered through the shared provenance kernel. The
contribution record's provenance never proves an attribution. Provenance is
deterministic for assistive technology: it is carried in the trigger's
accessible name (server-rendered), not only in a visual tooltip (verified by the
`toHaveAccessibleName` e2e test).

## 33. Shared-kernel reuse

`src/features/shared/temporal.ts` and `src/features/shared/provenance.ts` are
reused verbatim (no new date or provenance logic), and the M6.2 Timeline
parser/components are reused for the related-events projection.

## 34. Node Independence review

The schema, vocabularies, copy, and route are Node-neutral. The architecture
works unchanged for a research programme, a field station, a museum, a
university, a community association, an Indigenous organization, a field station
without legal personality, a museum collection, a government agency, an informal
network, a defunct institution, or a collective whose members are incompletely
documented. Node-specific difference is data, not a fork. Copy carries no
PDBFF/institution-specific language (unit-tested).

## 35. Accessibility

One h1 per page; logical heading hierarchy (h1 → section h2 → capacity h3 →
actor h4, no skipped levels); semantic lists; keyboard-operable links and the
provenance control; deterministic provenance in the accessible name; visible
focus; reduced-motion respected (shared primitives); light/dark parity; and no
serious/critical axe violations (Playwright axe scan).

## 36. Responsive and theme behavior

375px behavior, no horizontal overflow, no duplicate ids, no console/hydration
errors, and light/dark parity are covered by parameterized Playwright quality
tests.

## 38. pgTAP plan and result

`plan(77)` = 34 `throws_ok` (4-arg constraint/FK with native messages;
permission denials as `'42501', NULL`; one 3-arg app-auth message) + 4
`has_function` + 39 `is`. Each constraint fixture is unique in every other
respect so no unrelated constraint masks it; the plan count was reconciled
mechanically. Not runnable in the authoring environment (no local Postgres /
Docker); it is authoritative on the Mac (§53).

## 39. Vitest result

Three suites cover Node-neutral copy without ranking/impact/achievement
language, fail-closed parsing (dropping malformed sub-records), temporal and
provenance derivation via the shared kernels, contributor grouping by capacity,
the collective-attribution boundary, and honest empty states. Not runnable in
the authoring environment (the mounted `node_modules` is built for the user's
macOS, so the Linux native bindings for vitest/rolldown cannot load); it is
authoritative on the Mac.

## 40. Playwright result

## 46. Explicitly deferred scope

Universal Entity Engine; generalized polymorphic attribution; a
contribution↔event relation vocabulary; Outputs / Historical Records ingestion /
dataset or publication repository / citation graph; effectiveness or impact
scoring; contribution percentages; rankings; AI-generated attribution or
narrative; automated contributor extraction; duplicate resolution; public
editing / full editorial workflow; a distinct contributor/host/funder/custodian
typology beyond capacity; Node administration / federation / tenancy; M6.7.

## 47. Open ethical, governance, legal, and editorial questions

Community authorization and consent workflows for collective/Indigenous
contributions; governance of culturally restricted/sacred knowledge (recording a
historical role without exposure); the boundary between acknowledgment and
permission; editorial workflow and dispute handling for contested attributions;
whether and how outputs/records and their custody are later modelled (CC2); and
a possible finer contributor typology. These are recorded as deferred extension
points, not resolved in code.

## 48. Genuine compromises or limitations

`contribution_events` is a pure projection without a relation-type vocabulary
(deferred, to avoid forking the shared timeline read shape). `sort_order` exists
for stable, historically-neutral ordering and defaults to 0 (equal) — it is not
a ranking and must never be surfaced as one. The engine records historical roles
but cannot, in code, adjudicate community authorization or resolve
culturally-restricted-knowledge governance; where the current model cannot
truthfully represent a collective, it preserves an honest limitation rather than
inventing a person or institution.
